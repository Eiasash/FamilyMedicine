# Nerve Blocks Part I. Upper Extremity

**Citation:** Am Fam Physician. 2020;101(11):654-664
**Source:** American Family Physician
**Specialty:** כאב

## Abstract

Procedural anesthesia is administered by family physicians for a variety of conditions, including
 neuropathies, fracture reduction, foreign body removals, and complex wound management. A
 nerve block may be preferred because it provides effective regional anesthesia with less anesthetic.
 Nerve blocks require a thorough understanding of relevant anatomy, aiding the physician in opti-
 mizing the anesthesia effect while minimizing complications. Nerve blocks can be guided by bony
 landmarks, peripheral nerve stimulation, or ultrasonography. Ultrasound-guided nerve blocks are
 superior in decreasing procedural complications and procedure time. Physicians should be aware
 of these techniques to appropriately counsel their patients on procedural options. Nerve blocks of
 the ulnar, median, and radial nerves at the wrist and elbow provide effective anesthesia for a wide
 range of medical procedures in the upper extremity. (Am Fam Physician. 2020;101(11):654-664
