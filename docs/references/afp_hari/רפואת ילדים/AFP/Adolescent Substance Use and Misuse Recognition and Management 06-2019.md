# Adolescent Substance Use and Misuse Recognition and Management

**Citation:** Am Fam Physician. 2019;99(11):689-696
**Source:** American Family Physician
**Specialty:** רפואת ילדים

## Abstract

Adolescent use of illicit substances imposes an enormous burden on individuals, families, and communities. The types of
 illicit substances adolescents are using have changed drastically over the past decade with decreases in alcohol use (includ-
 ing binge alcohol use) offset by increases in electronic cigarette, marijuana, and opioid use. Primary care physicians have
 the opportunity to identify adolescents who use illicit substances. The U.S. Preventive Services Task Force and the American
 Academy of Family Physicians found insufficient evidence to assess the balance of benefits and harms of primary care–based
 behavioral interventions to prevent or reduce illicit substance use or nonmedical pharmaceutical use in children or adoles-
 cents. The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends that clinicians become familiar with Screening, Brief Intervention,
 and Referral to Treatment initiatives. Validated screening tools that may be used in primary care include the CRAFFT, POSIT,
 AUDIT, and NIAAA Screening Guide. During the clinical visit, a split-visit model encourages parents to participate in the visit
 for a limited time but also allows adolescents to have confidential conversations with physicians. Evidence-based treatment
 modalities range from school- and parent-based interventions to medication-assisted treatment. Brief interventions using
 components of motivational interviewing may be suitable for addressing substance use, even among adolescents not seeking
 treatment. Prevention efforts can supplement cessation programs to maximize program effectiveness. (Am Fam Physician.
